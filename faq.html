Frequently Asked Questions

Your questions about the FIFA World Cup, answered.

How often is the FIFA World Cup held?

The FIFA World Cup is held every four years. This schedule allows time for a comprehensive qualification process involving over 200 national teams from around the globe.

How many teams will be in the 2026 World Cup?

The 2026 World Cup will feature 48 teams, an increase from the 32-team format used in previous tournaments. This expansion is designed to make the tournament more inclusive.

Who is hosting the 2026 World Cup?

For the first time in history, the 2026 World Cup will be co-hosted by three countries: the United States, Canada, and Mexico. Matches will be played across 16 different cities in North America.

What is the "knockout stage"?

The knockout stage is a single-elimination tournament that follows the group stage. Teams play one match per round, and the winner advances while the loser is eliminated. This high-stakes format includes the Round of 32, Round of 16, Quarter-finals, Semi-finals, and the Final.

What is VAR?

VAR stands for Video Assistant Referee. It is a system where a team of officials reviews key decisions made by the on-field referee using video footage. It is used for clear and obvious errors related to goals, penalty decisions, direct red cards, and mistaken identity.

Where can I watch the World Cup?

The World Cup is one of the most widely broadcast sporting events in the world. Major television networks and streaming services in nearly every country hold broadcasting rights. Check your local listings to find the official broadcaster in your region.
